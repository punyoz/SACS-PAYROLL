# 1. Introduction to This Handbook

## 1.1 About this handbook

Welcome to Shepherd Angels Christian School of Antipolo, Inc. In this handbook, "the School" means Shepherd Angels Christian School of Antipolo, Inc. and all of its branches.

This handbook explains how we work together: what the School expects of you, what you can expect from the School, and how your attendance, pay and leave are handled. Please read it, keep it, and sign the acknowledgment form at the front.

- The handbook is a guide to School policy. It is not an employment contract. Your appointment or contract, the Labor Code of the Philippines and other laws come first if anything here is different.
- Where this handbook says nothing on a matter, the law applies.
- The School may change this handbook. Every change is made in writing and announced to all employees (see Section 11).
- Questions about anything in this handbook go to HR or to your school head.

# 2. Conditions of Employment

## 2.1 Equal opportunity

The School hires, pays, trains and promotes people on the basis of their qualifications and performance. The School does not discriminate because of sex, age, disability, civil status, ethnicity or any other ground the law protects. This follows the Labor Code, the Magna Carta of Women (RA 9710), the Magna Carta for Persons with Disability (RA 7277) and the Anti-Age Discrimination in Employment Act (RA 10911).

As a Christian school, the School may ask employees to respect and uphold its Christian identity at work (see Section 3.8).

## 2.2 Kinds of employment

| Status | What it means |
| Probationary | You are on trial for a set period. The School tells you the standards for becoming regular when you are hired. |
| Regular | You have passed probation, or you do work that is usual and necessary to the School. You have security of tenure. |
| Contractual / fixed-term | You are hired for a specific period or project stated in your contract. |
| Provisional | A status the School uses for staff whose papers or qualifications are still being completed [TO CONFIRM meaning] |

The system records each employee as Teaching or Non-Teaching, with one of the statuses above. Your category and status decide your leave credits (Section 7.2) and some pay rules.

## 2.3 Probation

- **Teaching staff:** probation lasts up to three (3) consecutive school years of satisfactory service, as the Manual of Regulations for Private Schools sets for full-time teachers.
- **Non-teaching staff:** probation lasts up to six (6) months from the first day of work (Labor Code, Art. 296).
- You become regular when you complete probation and meet the standards made known to you at hiring.

## 2.4 Security of tenure

A regular employee can be dismissed only for a just or authorized cause and only after due process (Section 8). Probationary employees may also be let go if they do not meet the standards made known to them at hiring.

## 2.5 Requirements before you start

Before or on your first day, give HR the documents your 201 file needs:

- birth certificate, and marriage certificate if married;
- SSS, PhilHealth and Pag-IBIG numbers, and your TIN;
- diplomas and transcript of records;
- for teachers: your PRC license (see Section 2.6);
- NBI clearance [TO CONFIRM];
- a pre-employment medical certificate [TO CONFIRM];
- two recent ID photos and your emergency contact.

HR enters your record in the system. You can see your own record under **My Profile**.

## 2.6 Licensed teachers

Teachers must hold a valid PRC license (RA 7836). HR records the license number and expiry date. The system reminds HR 60 days before a license expires. Renew it on time and give HR a copy of the new license.

## 2.7 Child protection

Every employee must protect learners from abuse, neglect, bullying, exploitation and discrimination. The School follows the Special Protection of Children Act (RA 7610) and the DepEd Child Protection Policy (DepEd Order No. 40, s. 2012).

- Report any sign or suspicion of child abuse to the school head or the Child Protection Committee at once, on the same day.
- Never investigate on your own and never confront the person suspected. Let the committee act.
- Keep every report confidential.

## 2.8 Professional boundaries with learners

Corporal punishment is never allowed (Family Code, Art. 233; RA 7610). This includes hitting, pinching, shaking, or making a learner do something painful or humiliating as punishment.

Using reasonable force only to stop a fight, to protect someone from harm, or to take away a dangerous object is not corporal punishment.

**Not allowed:**

- being alone with a learner in a closed room or off campus without the permission of the parent and the school head;
- giving a learner personal or intimate gifts;
- private messages to learners about anything other than school work;
- jokes, comments or stories of a sexual nature;
- touching that is not needed and not appropriate.

**Expected:**

- keep the door open when you meet a learner alone;
- use School channels for messages to learners and parents;
- tell your school head about any situation that could be misunderstood;
- praise and encourage learners without unnecessary physical contact.

## 2.9 Anti-sexual harassment and safe spaces

The School does not tolerate sexual harassment or gender-based harassment in any form, in person or online. This follows the Anti-Sexual Harassment Act (RA 7877) and the Safe Spaces Act (RA 11313).

- Harassment includes unwanted sexual remarks, advances, requests, jokes, gestures, touching and sharing of sexual images, and any work decision made in exchange for sexual favors.
- Complaints go to the School's Committee on Decorum and Investigation (CODI) [TO CONFIRM members] using the form in Appendix A. See Section 9.3.
- No one may be punished for filing a complaint in good faith or for being a witness.

## 2.10 Reporting wrongdoing

If you see something illegal, dishonest or against School policy, report it to your school head, to HR, or, if it involves them, to the School owner [TO CONFIRM]. Reports made in good faith are protected from retaliation. A report you know to be false is a serious offense.

## 2.11 First aid

Employees who work directly with learners should know basic first aid. The School will arrange first aid training [TO CONFIRM which positions and how often].

## 2.12 Drug-free and alcohol-free workplace

Bringing, using or being under the influence of illegal drugs or alcohol at work is not allowed (RA 9165; DOLE Department Order No. 53-03). The School may require drug testing as the law allows [TO CONFIRM].

## 2.13 Smoke-free campus

Smoking and vaping are not allowed anywhere on School grounds (RA 9211; Executive Order No. 26, s. 2017).

## 2.14 Data privacy and confidential information

The School follows the Data Privacy Act (RA 10173).

- Information about learners, parents and employees, including grades, addresses, health, pay and attendance, is confidential. Use it only for your work and share it only with people who need it.
- Do not copy School records to personal devices or personal accounts.
- The system shows you only your own pay and attendance. Do not use another person's account, and never share your password or your sign-in code.
- You may ask HR to see and correct the personal information the School keeps about you.

## 2.15 Conflict of interest

Tell your school head in writing about any situation where your personal interest may conflict with the School's, such as private tutoring of your own learners for pay, selling to the School, or supervising a relative.

# 4. The Workplace

## 4.1 Work schedule

Your working hours come from the schedule the system assigns to you, checked in this order:

- your own schedule, if HR gave you one;
- otherwise your level's schedule (Pre-school, Grade School, High School, Senior High School or Non-teaching);
- otherwise your branch's schedule;
- otherwise the School's default schedule: **8:00 AM to 5:00 PM, 8 working hours, Monday to Friday**.

Each schedule has a start time, an end time, the hours required, a grace period and the working days. HR or the Super Admin changes a schedule only from today or a later date. Days already recorded are never judged again under a new schedule.

## 4.2 Meal and rest breaks

You have a meal break of at least sixty (60) minutes a day (Labor Code, Art. 85) [TO CONFIRM the School's break times]. Short rest breaks of up to twenty (20) minutes are counted as working time. Teachers supervising learners at lunch get their meal break at another time [TO CONFIRM].

## 4.3 Lactation breaks

Nursing mothers get paid lactation breaks of at least forty (40) minutes in total for every eight (8) working hours, in addition to the meal break (RA 10028). The School provides a clean, private lactation area [TO CONFIRM location]. Tell HR or your school head so your schedule can make room for the breaks.

## 4.4 Attendance and RFID taps

Your attendance is recorded only by tapping your RFID card at your branch's kiosk.

- **Tap in** when you arrive and **tap out** when you leave. The first tap of the day is your time in; the next is your time out.
- **Never tap for someone else** or let someone tap for you. This is a serious offense (Section 8.2).
- A second tap within a few seconds, or again within 5 minutes, is not counted: the kiosk shows "Already recorded at…".
- The kiosk refuses a tap that is more than 3 hours before your shift starts, a card of another branch, a card that is not registered or is deactivated, and a tap on a day you are on approved leave.
- Report a lost or damaged card to your Admin at once. Until a new card is issued, ask your Admin how your attendance will be recorded.

**What each day is called in the system:**

| Status | When |
| On Time | You tapped in no later than your start time plus the grace period |
| Early Bird | You tapped in at least 15 minutes before your start time |
| Late | You tapped in after the grace period |
| Undertime | You tapped out before your shift ended |
| Absent (short day) | You worked less than half of your day's hours |
| Half Day | You have an approved half-day leave and worked the other half |
| Absent | You did not tap on a working day without approved leave |
| Incomplete | You tapped in but did not tap out |
| On Leave | You are on approved leave |
| Holiday | A holiday or a class/work suspension, and you did not work |

### Grace period and lateness

The grace period is **15 minutes** [TO CONFIRM] for everyone. Tapping in within the grace period is On Time. Once you are past the grace period, the **whole** lateness is recorded, not only the minutes after the grace period.

> Example: your shift starts at 8:00 AM. A tap at 8:12 AM is On Time. A tap at 8:20 AM is Late, and 20 minutes are recorded.

**Lateness is recorded but not deducted from your pay.** Undertime, short days and absences are deducted (Section 5.5). Repeated lateness is handled under the Rules of Conduct (Section 8.2), not through pay.

## 4.6 Attendance corrections

If a day is wrong, for example you forgot to tap out, ask for a correction under **My Attendance**:

- You may ask within **3 days** of the day to be corrected.
- You may ask up to **5 times a cutoff**.
- Days that can be corrected: Incomplete, Undertime, Half Day and Absent (short day).
- HR decides. An Incomplete day holds up your payroll until it is corrected, so ask quickly.

## 4.7 Absence without leave (AWOL)

If you are absent for **five (5) consecutive working days** without an approved or pending leave, the system opens an AWOL case:

- your pay is held while the case is open;
- HR sends you a **Notice to Return to Work and Explain**, which you will see under **My Profile > My Notices**;
- you have **5 days** to report back or explain in writing; you can reply in the system;
- if you do not reply, HR may send a **Notice of Separation** for abandonment of work, effective 3 days later unless you return or explain before then;
- returning to work, or a leave being approved for those days, closes the case and releases your pay.

This follows the due process in Section 8.3.

## 4.8 Overtime

Overtime is work beyond your schedule that HR approved **before** it is paid. Tapping out late does not by itself earn overtime.

- File overtime under **My Requests** at least **1 day before**, or up to **2 days after** as a late filing.
- You may file up to **3 hours a day** and **10 hours a cutoff**.
- Your Admin confirms the work; HR decides.
- You are paid for the smaller of the approved hours and the hours you actually worked past your shift end, at **125%** of your hourly rate (Labor Code, Art. 87). See Section 5.6.

## 4.9 Substitute teaching

When a teacher's leave is approved, the system proposes a substitution for each class day of the leave. HR picks the substitute (a teacher of the same level and branch who is at work and under the weekly limit) and approves it. Your Admin confirms the work.

- HR's approval of the substitution is the authorization. You do not file an overtime request for it.
- You are paid **only if your own RFID taps cover the class time**: a time in no later than the class start (within the grace period) and a time out no earlier than the class end.
- Pay is counted as overtime: each class period is counted as one hour (Section 5.7).
- A teacher may cover up to **5 substitute periods a week**.
- Time already paid as approved overtime is not paid again as substitution.

## 4.10 Holiday duty and special working days

You work on a holiday only when HR assigns you holiday duty or approves your holiday work. Holiday work without HR's assignment or approval is not paid as holiday work. On a special working day declared by the government, the School is open and you report as usual.

# 5. Wages and Benefits

## 5.1 How your pay is set

Your **monthly salary** is set in your appointment. The system works out your other rates from it:

- **Daily rate** = monthly salary × 12 ÷ 261 (the working days in a year);
- **Hourly rate** = daily rate ÷ 8.

> Example used in this section: a monthly salary of ₱26,100. Daily rate = 26,100 × 12 ÷ 261 = **₱1,200**. Hourly rate = 1,200 ÷ 8 = **₱150**.

Salary changes are made by HR or the Super Admin as a new version that starts from a future pay period, always with a reason. No one changes their own salary.

## 5.2 Pay periods, cutoffs and paydays

You are paid twice a month.

| Payslip | Payslip date | If that day is a weekend or holiday |
| 1st half (1–15) | the 15th | the next working day |
| 2nd half (16–end) | the last day of the month | the working day before |

Pay is released on the payslip date [TO CONFIRM release method: payroll account or cash].

Each payslip counts your attendance up to the **day before** its payslip date. That day is counted in the next payslip. No day is counted twice or skipped.

> Example: the November 1–15 payslip date is Monday, November 16 (the 15th was a Sunday), so it counts attendance from October 30 to November 15.

You can see a payslip in **My Payslips** once HR approves the payroll.

## 5.3 The 1–15 payslip

Your 1–15 payslip pays **half of your monthly salary, less that cutoff's attendance deductions** (absences, undertime, short days, unpaid leave). It has no SSS, PhilHealth, Pag-IBIG, tax or loan deductions.

## 5.4 The 16–end payslip

Your 16–end payslip pays the other half of your monthly salary, less its own attendance deductions. It also:

- adds the month's overtime, holiday pay, substitution pay, Early Bird incentive, additional pay and any approved correction;
- deducts the whole month's **SSS, PhilHealth and Pag-IBIG**, and **withholding tax** computed on the whole month's taxable pay;
- deducts cash advance installments and SSS / Pag-IBIG loan amortizations.

If deductions are more than what the payslip can pay, it is paid as ₱0.00 and the balance carries to the next month.

## 5.5 Attendance deductions

| Item | Deduction |
| Absent | 100% of your daily rate for each day |
| Undertime | Minutes not worked ÷ 60 × hourly rate |
| Absent (short day) | Only the time not worked, at the hourly rate (never a full day) |
| Half day of unpaid leave | 50% of your daily rate |
| Leave without pay | Your daily rate for each day |
| Late | **Not deducted** (recorded only; see Section 4.4) |
| Holiday, suspension, approved paid leave | Not deducted |

> Examples (₱26,100 salary, ₱150 an hour). Tapping out at 4:30 PM on an 8:00 to 5:00 schedule: 30 minutes of undertime = **₱75**. Working only 3 of 8 hours: Absent (short day), 5 hours not worked = **₱750**. One unexcused absence: **₱1,200**.

Attendance deductions in a month are never more than the monthly salary. Days not yet resolved (Incomplete, Pending Correction) are not counted either way until they are fixed; they hold up your payroll until then.

## 5.6 Overtime, holiday and rest-day pay

| Work | Pay |
| Approved overtime on a regular working day | 125% of your hourly rate |
| Regular holiday, worked (assigned or approved) | 200% of your daily rate for the day (your salary plus 100%) |
| Special non-working day, worked | 130% (your salary plus 30%) |
| Rest day, worked | 130% |
| Regular holiday that falls on your rest day, worked | 260% |
| Special day that falls on your rest day, worked | 150% |
| Holiday or special day not worked | No deduction: your monthly salary already covers it |

Holiday pay is counted by the hours worked, up to 8 hours. These rates follow the Labor Code (Arts. 87, 93 and 94) and the DOLE holiday pay advisory for the year.

> Example: HR approved 2 hours of overtime and you worked 1 hour 30 minutes past your shift end. Paid: 1.5 × ₱150 × 125% = **₱281.25**. Working 8 hours on Bonifacio Day (regular holiday) as assigned holiday duty adds **₱1,200** on top of your salary.

## 5.7 Substitution pay

Substitution pay = periods covered × 1 hour × your hourly rate × 125%. On a holiday the holiday rate × 130% applies; on your rest day, 130%. It is paid only when your taps cover the class time (Section 4.9).

> Example: you substitute for 2 periods, from 1:00 to 3:00 PM, and your taps show you were in from 7:45 AM to 5:05 PM. Pay: 2 × 1 × ₱150 × 125% = **₱375**. If you had tapped out at 2:30 PM, your taps would not cover the class time and it would not be paid; HR is told.

## 5.8 Government contributions

The School deducts your share of these contributions, adds its own share, and remits them. All three are deducted in full on the **16–end payslip** each month.

| Contribution | Your share | School's share | Basis |
| SSS | 5% of your monthly salary credit (₱5,000 to ₱35,000) | 10%, plus the Employees' Compensation premium | RA 11199; SSS schedule from January 2025 |
| PhilHealth | 2.5% of your monthly basic salary (computed on ₱10,000 to ₱100,000) | 2.5% | RA 11223; 5% premium |
| Pag-IBIG | 2% of your monthly salary, up to ₱10,000 (maximum ₱200); 1% if you earn ₱1,500 or less | 2% | RA 9679; HDMF Circular No. 460 |

> Example (₱26,100 salary): SSS 5% of the ₱26,000 salary credit = **₱1,300**; PhilHealth 2.5% of ₱26,100 = **₱652.50**; Pag-IBIG **₱200**. Total: **₱2,152.50** a month.

These rates are set by law and by the agencies. When they change, the School applies the new rates from the date the agency sets [TO CONFIRM WITH HR/LEGAL each year].

## 5.9 Withholding tax

Income tax is withheld once a month, on the 16–end payslip, using the BIR monthly withholding tax table (TRAIN law, RA 10963). It is computed on the month's taxable pay: gross pay, less attendance deductions, less non-taxable items, less your SSS, PhilHealth and Pag-IBIG.

> Example (₱26,100 salary, no other items): taxable pay = 26,100 − 2,152.50 = ₱23,947.50. Tax = 15% of the amount over ₱20,833 = **₱467.18**. The 16–end payslip then pays 13,050 − 2,152.50 − 467.18 = **₱10,430.32**.

At the end of the year the School computes the tax due on your whole year's pay. Any difference is collected or refunded on a 16–end payslip, usually December 16–31. You receive BIR Form 2316 from the School.

## 5.10 Early Bird incentive

The School gives an Early Bird incentive to **one employee each pay period**: the person with the most early time-ins.

- An early time-in is a tap at least **15 minutes before your own start time** (8:00 AM start: 7:45 counts, 7:46 does not).
- You are not eligible if you have an unexcused absence in that pay period. Approved leave does not count against you.
- Ties go to the earliest average arrival, then the fewest late days, then the fewest absences.
- **HR approves** the incentive before it is paid. It appears on the winner's 16–end payslip as "Early Bird Incentive" and is taxable.
- **Amount: as announced by the School each year.**

## 5.11 Additional pay

The Accountant records additional pay such as incentives, overload, honoraria and bonuses. HR approves each item, and it is paid on the 16–end payslip of the month it belongs to. Each type is marked taxable or non-taxable.

## 5.12 Licensed-teacher subsidy

Licensed teachers who qualify receive a yearly subsidy.

- It is earned month by month and paid in the payout month the School sets.
- You may ask for an advance up to the amount you have earned so far.
- If you resign, you receive the part you have earned. If you are dismissed for cause, the unpaid subsidy is forfeited.
- The Accountant processes it and HR approves it.
- **Amount: as announced by the School each year.**

## 5.13 13th month pay

Every employee who worked at least one month in the year receives 13th month pay (PD 851):

**13th month pay = total basic salary actually earned in the year ÷ 12**

Absences and leave without pay reduce your basic salary earned. Paid leave, lateness, undertime, overtime and incentives do not change it. It is paid in December as a separate payout, no later than December 24.

> Example: ₱26,100 × 12 months = ₱313,200. With 2 unpaid absences (₱2,400): (313,200 − 2,400) ÷ 12 = **₱25,900**.

## 5.14 The ₱90,000 tax-free ceiling

Your 13th month pay and other benefits (such as the licensed-teacher subsidy) are tax-free up to **₱90,000** a calendar year (TRAIN law). Only the amount above ₱90,000 is taxed.

## 5.15 Cash advances

The School does not lend money. It may give you a **cash advance** against your salary.

- The Accountant files the advance and HR approves it.
- **Limit:** what you still owe on cash advances, counting only the amount borrowed (never interest), may not be more than **one month of your basic salary**, and never more than ₱50,000.
- It is deducted in installments on your 16–end payslips until it is paid.
- An installment never brings your net pay below ₱0; any unpaid part stays on your balance.
- You can see your advances under **My Payslips > Deductions & Other Pay > My deductions**.

> Example: salary ₱26,100. You still owe ₱10,000 on an earlier advance. You may borrow up to ₱16,100 more. A ₱6,000 advance at ₱2,000 per 16–end payslip is repaid in three months.

## 5.16 SSS and Pag-IBIG loans

Salary and other loans you take from SSS or Pag-IBIG are deducted from your pay as the agency instructs.

- **HR encodes** the loan from the agency's deduction notice: the agency, the reference number and the amount per 16–end payslip.
- These loans have no School limit; the agency sets the amount.
- HR never encodes its own loan; another HR or the Super Admin does.
- The deduction appears on your 16–end payslip with the agency, the remaining balance and the reference number. You also see it under **My deductions**.

## 5.17 Other deductions

The School deducts from your pay only what the law requires or allows (Labor Code, Art. 113), or what you authorized in writing. Each deduction appears as its own line on your payslip.

## 5.18 Tenure Pay Protection

Some long-serving employees have a signed contract clause that protects their pay from certain deductions, such as absences, lateness, holidays, suspensions, summer break or Christmas break.

- It applies only when HR has granted it to you, the policy for your branch is active, and you have the minimum years of service the policy requires.
- Only the kinds of days the policy covers are protected.
- A protected deduction is shown on your payslip as **waived**, with the grant it came from.

## 5.19 Health and social benefits

- **PhilHealth:** hospital and medical benefits for you and your dependents.
- **SSS:** sickness, maternity, disability, retirement, death and funeral benefits, and SSS loans.
- **Employees' Compensation (EC):** benefits for work-related sickness, injury or death, through SSS (PD 626).
- **Pag-IBIG:** savings, housing loans and short-term loans.
- **HMO:** [TO CONFIRM whether the School provides an HMO and for whom.]

## 5.20 Questions and corrections on your payslip

Check your payslip every pay period.

- If you think a payslip is wrong, tell **HR or the Accountant in writing within fifteen (15) days** of receiving it [TO CONFIRM], using the form in Appendix C. Say which payslip, which line, and why.
- An approved payslip is never changed. If there is an error, the Accountant files a **correction**, HR approves it, and it is settled on a later payslip: an underpayment is paid to you, an overpayment is recovered from a later 16–end payslip.
- Every correction shows its reason, and who approved it.

## 7.2 Leave credits

Vacation, sick and emergency leave come from **one leave pool** each year:

| Your status | Paid leave days a year |
| Regular | 5 days |
| Probationary, provisional or contractual | 3 days |

- Credits are prorated from your hire date, and from the date your status changes. They are rounded down to the half day.
- After 12 months of service you earn the Regular rate (5 days) from your service anniversary. This meets the Service Incentive Leave of the Labor Code (Art. 95).
- Your balance appears in **My Requests** and in the Leave Balance box on your payslip.

> Example: a regular employee hired on July 1 earns 5 × 184 ÷ 365 = 2.52 days for that year, rounded down to **2.5 days**.

## 7.3 How to file leave

File every leave under **My Requests**.

| Kind of leave | When to file |
| Vacation leave (whole day) | At least **7 days before** the first day of leave |
| Half-day leave (morning or afternoon) | At least **1 day before**, or up to **2 days after** as a late filing |
| Sick and emergency leave | Notify on the same day; see Section 7.4 |
| Bereavement leave | As soon as you can; see Section 7.11 |

- Choose **with pay** or **without pay**. Attach proof (PDF, PNG or JPG, under 2 MB) when needed.
- Your Admin endorses your request and HR decides. You see the decision and the reason in My Requests.
- If you ask for more paid days than you have left, HR's approval pays the days your balance covers and the rest is **leave without pay**.
- A paid leave day shows on your payslip as "Paid leave (N days)" inside your basic pay; it is not deducted.

## 7.4 Sick and emergency leave

- Notify HR or your school head **as early as possible on the same day**.
- For an illness of **three (3) or more consecutive days**, give HR a medical certificate when you return [TO CONFIRM].
- Sick and emergency leave use your leave pool. When the pool is used up, the days are leave without pay.

## 7.5 Leave without pay

You may ask for leave without pay for personal reasons. It must be approved in advance, except in an emergency. Each day is deducted at your daily rate, and it reduces your 13th month pay.

## 7.6 Maternity leave

Under the Expanded Maternity Leave Law (RA 11210), a woman employee is entitled to:

- **105 days** of paid maternity leave for a live birth, whatever her civil status;
- **15 additional days** if she is a solo parent;
- **60 days** for a miscarriage or emergency termination of pregnancy;
- an option to extend for **30 days without pay**;
- an option to give up to **7 days** of her leave to the child's father or an alternate caregiver [TO CONFIRM WITH HR/LEGAL].

The SSS maternity benefit is paid through the School, and the School pays the salary differential the law requires (unless the School is exempt). Notify HR as soon as you know, and give HR the SSS maternity notification. File the leave in My Requests.

## 7.7 Paternity leave

A married male employee is entitled to **7 days** of paid paternity leave for each of the first four deliveries of his lawful wife (RA 8187). File it in My Requests with the child's birth certificate.

## 7.8 Solo parent leave

An employee with a valid Solo Parent ID who has worked at least **six (6) months** is entitled to **7 working days** of paid parental leave a year (RA 8972 as amended by RA 11861). Give HR a copy of your Solo Parent ID and tell HR in writing when you need the leave [TO CONFIRM: filing in My Requests is not available yet].

## 7.9 Special leave for women

A woman employee who has worked at least **six (6) months** in the last twelve months is entitled to up to **two (2) months** of paid special leave after surgery for a gynecological disorder (Magna Carta of Women, RA 9710). Give HR the medical certificate and tell HR in writing [TO CONFIRM: filing in My Requests is not available yet].

## 7.10 VAWC leave

A woman employee who is a victim of violence, or whose child is, is entitled to up to **10 days** of paid leave, which may be extended when a court order requires it (Anti-Violence Against Women and Their Children Act, RA 9262). Give HR a certification from the barangay, the prosecutor or the court. HR keeps the matter confidential [TO CONFIRM: filing in My Requests is not available yet].

## 7.11 Bereavement leave

The School gives up to **3 days** of paid bereavement leave for each death of a spouse, parent, child or sibling. File it in My Requests with the relationship and the date of death. It does not need 7 days' notice and does not use your leave pool.

## 7.12 Unused leave conversion

Unused paid leave in the pool may be converted to cash at your daily rate, as the School decides each year [TO CONFIRM]. Up to **10 days** a year of converted leave is tax-free; any excess is taxable (BIR de minimis rules). Unused leave is also included in your final pay when you leave the School [TO CONFIRM].

## 7.13 Sickness and disability benefits

If you are sick or injured for more than three days and have used your leave, you may claim the SSS sickness benefit through the School. For work-related sickness or injury, the Employees' Compensation program applies. HR helps you file.

## 7.14 Coming back from leave

After a long illness or a maternity leave, HR may ask for a medical certificate that you are fit to work [TO CONFIRM]. You return to your former position, or one of the same rank and pay.

## 8.3 Due process

Before any suspension or dismissal for a just cause, the School follows the twin-notice rule (Labor Code, Art. 292; DOLE Department Order No. 147-15):

- **First notice (Notice to Explain):** states the specific acts you are charged with and the rule broken, and gives you at least **five (5) calendar days** to answer in writing [TO CONFIRM WITH HR/LEGAL].
- **Hearing or conference:** you may explain, present evidence and bring a representative. A hearing is held if you ask for one in writing, if the facts are disputed, or if the School decides one is needed.
- **Second notice (Notice of Decision):** states the decision, the reasons, and when it takes effect.

For dismissal for an authorized cause, such as redundancy or closure, the School gives you and DOLE written notice at least **30 days** before, and pays the separation pay the law requires (Labor Code, Arts. 298–299).

## 8.4 Off-duty conduct and second jobs

What you do outside work is your own affair, unless it harms learners, the School's name, or your ability to do your job. Tell your school head in writing before taking a second job that may conflict with your schedule or your duties [TO CONFIRM].

## 8.5 Resignation

Give the School written notice at least **thirty (30) days** before your last day (Labor Code, Art. 300). Teachers are asked to time their resignation so a class is not left without a teacher during the school year [TO CONFIRM].

## 8.6 Final pay

When you leave the School, the Accountant computes your final pay and HR approves it. It is released **within 30 days** of your last day (DOLE Labor Advisory No. 06, s. 2020), after clearance [TO CONFIRM clearance process]. Final pay includes:

- unpaid salary up to your last day;
- your prorated 13th month pay;
- the licensed-teacher subsidy you earned and were not yet paid (if you qualify);
- unused leave, if the School converts it (Section 7.12);
- less SSS, PhilHealth, Pag-IBIG and withholding tax;
- less unpaid cash advances, if you signed the final-pay authority.

Final pay is never below ₱0. Any amount it cannot cover stays on your balance for collection.

## 8.7 Certificate of employment and references

The School issues a Certificate of Employment within **3 days** of your request (DOLE Labor Advisory No. 06, s. 2020). Only HR or the school head may give references about current or former employees.

# 9. Complaints and Grievances

## 9.1 Open door

You may bring any concern about your work to your school head or to HR at any time. Raising a concern in good faith never counts against you.

## 9.2 Grievance steps

- **Step 1:** talk to your immediate supervisor or school head within 7 days of the matter [TO CONFIRM].
- **Step 2:** if it is not settled, put it in writing to HR using the form in Appendix B. HR answers within 10 working days [TO CONFIRM].
- **Step 3:** if it is still not settled, write to the School owner, whose decision is final within the School [TO CONFIRM].

If your complaint is about your school head or HR, you may go directly to the next step.

## 9.3 Harassment complaints

Complaints of sexual or gender-based harassment go to the Committee on Decorum and Investigation (CODI) using the form in Appendix A. The CODI investigates, hears both sides, and recommends action, as RA 7877 and RA 11313 require.

## 9.4 Complaints from parents or others about an employee

The school head looks into the complaint, talks with the people involved, and tells the employee about it. Any discipline follows due process (Section 8.3).

## 9.5 Confidentiality and no retaliation

Complaints are kept as confidential as the investigation allows. No one may be punished for complaining or testifying in good faith.
